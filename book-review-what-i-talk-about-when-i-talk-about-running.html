Review: What I Talk About When I Talk About Running

By Denis Tarasenko Apr 25, 2025

I believe that the most profound thing books can do to us is inspire us to take some sort of action in order to improve our lives and those around us.

I don’t usually read self-help books and memoirs because they seem to me, for which reason I can’t fully grasp at the moment, a bit artificial and don’t bring “real” value per se.

When you look at bookstores these days, you’ll find memoirs of people from every domain imaginable to be on the most visible spots. Politicians, sportsmen, singers, actors, tv show hosts - you get the picture. It’d be OK if they only paved their way to the top spots in bookstores by cash and influence, but that’s not the worst. Many memoirs were ghostwrited. It makes me a bit uneasy even thinking about it. The sole purpose of these books becomes driving more sales for whatever product a memoir’s hero, or his company, pitches.

So that’s probably the reason why I don’t read memoirs and autobiographies.

But, fortunately, this book came out before this terrible trend and it is really what it claims to be.

Getting back to the beginning, books are supposed to make us take good action.

And when I started reading the book, I began running. Not just for the sake of it, but because I felt like doing it and it felt great doing something hard like this again. Social medias tend to tell different stories about us as humans, in a subconscious way, and we forget who we are over time. What I realized a couple of months ago is that I need a physical pain to place a puzzle into the happiness game every one of us is playing. I suffer mentally and physically when I spend the whole day indoors without doing anything to stress my body. But when I stress it, cause it deliberate pain, instead of feeling worse, I feel relieved and powerful.

And lemme tell you - this is very hard to drill it into our brain until you actually do it. Our minds are tricky fellows and they always are going to tell us that physical, and mental as well, effort is unnecessary at the moment until you constantly convince it otherwise. It is like muscles, they need to be reminded to do the job well.

I liked the book “What I talk when I talk about running”. It helped me open my eyes. Maybe right now it doesn’t seem like a very big change, but it will in the long-term.

I can see why people might dislike it. It contains too much real life, to put it mildly. Many memoirs are structured like hollywood movies, with uplifting climax in the end in order to please a reader as much as possible. This book, though, at first glance seemed that way too, but then the realify comes knocking and you get a completely different narrative. It doesn’t mean we have to give up. On the contrary. We have to embrace that life can put sticks in wheels from time to time, but we never should give up because the so-called good stuff always awaits us somewhere. Losers are only those who stop fighting. Other group always consists of winners.

In any case, I’ve just finished it and it’s my fresh impression. I’ll definitely spend some more time thinking about the book, soak it in to find even more lessons and wisdom, and get back taking new notes in my journal.
