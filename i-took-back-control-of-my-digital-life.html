I took back control of my digital life

By Denis Tarasenko May 25, 2026

Hey friends,

A couple of months ago I became obsessed with data ownership and privacy, so I gradually cut off from the big tech and started self-hosting basically everything. It included my websites, databases, analytics, photos, music, contacts, calendar, passwords, drive, and more. It took me quite a while to move all the pieces, but man it felt great when I realized that I'm in full charge of my stuff from that moment on, and I simply couldn't believe that it wasn't not a norm anymore.

And the best part is that it's quite easy to do. In my case, I just got a server on Hetzner, installed Coolify there, and started tinkering around until I moved everything. Recently I found a very intuitive step-by-step guide on how to self-host your most important stuff. No more subscriptions needed.

Believe me, once you get on this lane, there's no going back because it feels too good to have this kind of freedom. Everybody uses big tech's cloud because their pitch is too tempting to pass by (just give us all your data, we'll take care of it, don't you worry), but also most people are just ignorant and don't realize that there's another way.

I like the way Derek Sivers puts it:

You can ignore all the companies offering “solutions”, even if they are free, because they take away self-reliance. The point is to know how to do it yourself, not to have somebody do it for you. It’s worth a little up-front work, like learning how to drive.

Wish you happy self-hosting,

Denis
